Understanding research readiness

This section provides explanatory guidance, not a current readiness assessment.

Coverage concerns the requested research scope: what the research is intended to cover. Missing evidence concerns what remains necessary under reviewed readiness definitions to evaluate that scope.

Research readiness does not establish strategy edge or real-market performance.

Questions to ask when reviewing evidence

Use these questions when reviewing research evidence. This page does not report whether these questions have been answered.

  • Scope: Which instruments, date range and data frequency does the research cover?
  • Source: Where did the data come from, and is the evidence based on fixtures or market observations?
  • Gaps: What evidence is missing for the requested scope, and what conclusions remain unsupported?
  • Limits: What does the evidence demonstrate, and what would still be needed to assess strategy performance?

Passing fixture tests verifies behavior on those fixtures; it does not demonstrate real-market performance.

Record enough to revisit a result

This checklist provides explanatory guidance for reviewing research evidence, not a current assessment or an additional readiness requirement.

  • Record the dataset identifier or snapshot, source and retrieval or as-of date when available.
  • Record the code revision and research configuration, including parameters and any random seed.
  • Record the command or procedure used and a reference to the resulting output.
  • Explicitly identify unavailable details and explain how they limit reproducibility.

Reproducibility alone does not establish data readiness, strategy edge or real-market performance.

Before interpreting strategy results

These are explanatory questions, not a current assessment or an additional readiness requirement. Reproducible historical results can still overstate strategy performance when they use future information, reuse evaluation data to select strategies or omit trading costs.

  • Was each input available at the time the simulated decision was made, without using future observations?
  • Does the instrument universe reflect what was available at each historical date, including instruments later delisted, rather than only those that survive today?
  • How are splits, dividends and other corporate actions represented in prices and returns, and are those treatments consistent with the stated return calculation?
  • Was evaluation data kept separate from data used to select or tune the strategy?
  • How many strategies or parameter choices were tried, and was evaluation data reused to choose among them?
  • Do reported results account for fees, spread and slippage, and what execution assumptions remain untested?

These questions do not establish strategy edge or guarantee future performance.
