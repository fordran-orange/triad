Understanding research readiness

This section provides explanatory guidance, not a current readiness assessment.

Coverage concerns the requested research scope: what the research is intended to cover. Missing evidence concerns what remains necessary under reviewed readiness definitions to evaluate that scope.

Research readiness does not establish strategy edge or real-market performance.

Questions to ask when reviewing evidence

Use these questions when reviewing research evidence. This page does not report whether these questions have been answered.

  • Scope: Which instruments, date range and data frequency does the research cover?
  • Source: Where did the data come from, and is the evidence based on fixtures or market observations?
  • Gaps: What evidence is missing for the requested scope, and what conclusions remain unsupported?
  • Limits: What does the evidence demonstrate, and what would still be needed to assess strategy performance?

Passing fixture tests verifies behavior on those fixtures; it does not demonstrate real-market performance.

Record enough to revisit a result

This checklist provides explanatory guidance for reviewing research evidence, not a current assessment or an additional readiness requirement.

  • Record the dataset identifier or snapshot, source and retrieval or as-of date when available.
  • Record the code revision and research configuration, including parameters and any random seed.
  • Record the command or procedure used and a reference to the resulting output.
  • Explicitly identify unavailable details and explain how they limit reproducibility.

Reproducibility alone does not establish data readiness, strategy edge or real-market performance.

Before interpreting strategy results

These are explanatory questions, not a current assessment or an additional readiness requirement. Reproducible historical results can still overstate strategy performance when they use future information, reuse evaluation data to select strategies or omit trading costs.

  • Was each input available at the time the simulated decision was made, without using future observations?
  • Does the instrument universe reflect what was available at each historical date, including instruments later delisted, rather than only those that survive today?
  • How are splits, dividends and other corporate actions represented in prices and returns, and are those treatments consistent with the stated return calculation?
  • Was evaluation data kept separate from data used to select or tune the strategy?
  • How many strategies or parameter choices were tried, and was evaluation data reused to choose among them?
  • Do reported results account for fees, spread and slippage, and what execution assumptions remain untested?
  • What losses and peak-to-trough drawdowns occurred over the evaluated period, how long did recovery take, and were any drawdowns still unrecovered at the end?
  • What position sizes, leverage and concentration produced the reported returns, and how did those exposures change over the evaluated period?

Historical losses and drawdowns do not bound future losses. These questions do not establish strategy edge or guarantee future performance.

Round-trip trading-cost sensitivity

Enter a hypothetical round trip in USD. All assumptions are required, including explicit zero costs. One basis point is 0.01%.

These are additive estimates against unchanged reference prices, not simulated fills. Commissions use reference notionals. Spread is a full spread charged as half on each leg. Supplied prices must exclude these modeled costs to avoid double counting. The 1x, 2x and 3x scenarios mechanically scale all supplied costs; they do not forecast market conditions.

Financing, short borrow, taxes, liquidity, market impact, partial fills and corporate actions are excluded. Return on entry notional is net P&L divided by quantity times reference entry price, expressed as a percentage; it is not a portfolio or margin return. Closed-trade ledger analysis

User-supplied closed-trade analysis. The balance curve includes realized P&L only, not mark-to-market equity or intratrade drawdown. Cash flows and open positions are excluded. Overlapping trades are accepted but do not establish capital/margin feasibility or that the initial balance could finance them. Costs are deducted once exactly as supplied; the hypothetical calculator's cost model is not applied. This does not validate a strategy. Inputs are not saved.

Supply 1–2000 completed matched round trips, at most 1,000,000 UTF-8 bytes. CSV must contain exactly these columns in any order: trade_id,symbol,direction,quantity,entry_price,exit_price,entry_time,exit_time,total_cost. Standard quoted fields and a leading BOM are accepted. IDs must be unique (1–64 letters, digits, underscores or hyphens); symbols use 1–20 uppercase letters, digits, dots, underscores or hyphens. Direction is LONG or SHORT. Timestamps require ISO-8601 dates and times with a timezone, up to microsecond precision; entry must not follow exit.

Numbers use ordinary decimal notation with up to eight fractional digits and 32 characters, without exponents. Quantity and prices must be positive and at most 1,000,000. All-in total_cost is nonnegative USD, at most 1,000,000. Initial balance is positive USD, at most 1,000,000,000,000.

Entry notional exposure is an additive extension to the triad.trade-ledger.v1 report and JSON download. It is reconstructed exposure from supplied completed records: quantity times entry price, held constant on the half-open interval [entry_time, exit_time). Zero-duration trades are excluded from exposure but retained in P&L and trade counts. Simultaneous UTC entries and exits are grouped before reporting each state. Opposite directions do not cancel gross exposure.

Exposure excludes open positions and price changes; it is not mark-to-market risk and does not establish margin feasibility or executable portfolio risk. The ratio is entry notional divided by initial balance, not leverage or capital required. Amounts are USD decimal strings; ratios are dimensionless.

Per-symbol breakdown is an additive extension to the triad.trade-ledger.v1 report and JSON download. It groups realized-P&L-only results by symbol; it does not establish capital/margin feasibility, correlation or diversification, and does not validate a strategy.

User-supplied OHLCV inspection

Paste user-supplied completed price bars. This input is user-supplied, not a market feed, not a chart, not a backtest, and not strategy edge. Rows are checked for structure and summarized; prices are not adjusted for corporate actions, returns and signals are not computed, and nothing here places orders or reads a live account.

CSV columns, in any order, are exactly timestamp,symbol,open,high,low,close,volume. At most 5,000 data rows and 1,000,000 UTF-8 bytes. Symbols are 1–20 characters matching uppercase letters, digits, dots, underscores, or hyphens. Timestamps are timezone-aware ISO-8601 values and are reported in UTC. Prices are positive ordinary decimals and volume is nonnegative; each is at most 1,000,000, with at most eight fractional digits and no exponents. High must be at least the maximum of open, low, and close, and low must be at most the minimum of open and close. Duplicate symbol and timestamp pairs are rejected.
